<Article id="why" title="Why this matters for your self-model">

Your simulator is an integrator: it turns $\ddot\theta$ into $\dot\theta$ into $\theta$, one small sum at a time. Integrals also give you the energy the motor pumps into the arm per cycle (work = $\int \tau\,d\theta$), which is the cleanest way to see *why* resonance builds amplitude, and why the mass bump changes how much energy gets in. Riemann sums, the fundamental theorem, and numerical integration are the theory behind the integrators you've already been writing.

</Article>
