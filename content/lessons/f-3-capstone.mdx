<Article id="brief" title="The brief">

**Research question.** Under realistic excitation and noise, how do classical change detectors (CUSUM, GLR on model residuals) compare with learned detectors at finding body changes, measured as detection delay vs. false-alarm rate?

**Why a lab cares.** Fault and change detection is a safety requirement for robots working around people, and there's no small, standard benchmark for it on articulated robots. A clean, open benchmark with proper metrics (average run length to false alarm, detection delay) is something a lab can actually reuse. That's the most valuable kind of portfolio artifact.

**What you'll build.**
1. A benchmark generator in **MuJoCo**: the compliant pendulum plus one multi-joint system, with injected faults (mass change, friction increase, motor gain loss, stiffness drop), random fault times, and several excitation regimes.
2. Residual generators: the A1 least-squares self-model, the A4 frequency model, and optionally the F1 world model.
3. **CUSUM** and **GLR** on residuals, tuned for a target false-alarm rate.
4. A learned detector (e.g. a small 1-D CNN on residual windows) as a baseline.
5. Metrics: detection delay vs. average run length to false alarm, plus per-fault-type breakdowns.

**Deliverables.** A repo with a `pip install`-able benchmark package, a leaderboard table, and a 5-page paper.

</Article>

# One-sided CUSUM for an upward mean shift in a residual r_t
g = 0.0; alarms = []
for t, r in enumerate(residuals):
    g = max(0.0, g + r - nu)      # nu: drift (half the shift you care about)
    if g > h: alarms.append(t); g = 0.0   # h: threshold, set for the target false-alarm rate

**Rubric:**

1. **Benchmark design:** fault types, excitation and metrics are well motivated and documented.
2. **Correct statistics:** CUSUM/GLR calibrated properly, with run-length metrics used correctly.
3. **Fair comparison:** learned and classical detectors evaluated on identical splits.
