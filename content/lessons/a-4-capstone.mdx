<Article id="brief" title="The brief">

**Research question.** Can a robot keep an up-to-date frequency-domain self-model (natural frequency, damping ratio, gain) of a compliant joint while it operates, and how fast and how accurately does that model follow a body change?

**Why a lab cares.** Frequency-response models are how hardware teams characterize actuators and tune controllers. A lightweight online estimator of $\omega_n$ and $\zeta$ is directly useful for health monitoring and adaptive control on series-elastic joints. It's also a physics-grounded counterpart to the learned detectors in world-model work.

**What you'll build.**
1. Chirp and multi-sine excitation of the **MuJoCo** pendulum; a Bode plot estimated with `scipy.signal` (Welch / cross-spectral density).
2. A fitted second-order transfer function; $\omega_n$, $\zeta$ and DC gain compared with theory and with A1's time-domain estimates.
3. An online tracker: either a phase-locked loop that holds the −90° point, or recursive least squares on a discrete ARX model.
4. Mass bump mid-run: tracking delay and error vs. tracker bandwidth.
5. A comparison of the frequency-domain and time-domain self-models.

**Deliverables.** A repo plus a 4-page paper.

</Article>
