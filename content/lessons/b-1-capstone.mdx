<Article id="brief" title="The brief">

**Research question.** Why does an amplitude-based body-change detector on a compliant joint score far *below* chance, and which detector design avoids the frequency-dependent sign flip?

**Why a lab cares.** "Something changed about my body" is one of the most common signals a deployed robot needs: payload pickup, wear, damage. Detectors that look at response amplitude are the obvious first try. Showing rigorously that their sign depends on drive frequency relative to $\sqrt{g/L}$ and the shifted resonance, with transients and window length as confounders, is a real negative result that saves teams from a subtle trap. It's also *your* result, which makes it the strongest portfolio item you can have.

**What you'll build.**
1. A sweep in **MuJoCo** (the A1 model): for each drive frequency, runs with and without a ×1.5 mass bump, several bump phases each, and sensor noise.
2. The map of $\Delta$amplitude vs. frequency, with theory overlaid ($\omega^2 > g/L$ band, both resonances).
3. ROC curves and AUC for three detectors: the naive amplitude score, $|\Delta|$, and a model-residual detector (the A1 self-model's prediction error, or its refitted $k/I$).
4. AUC as a function of the *test-frequency distribution*, showing how the same detector can score 0.1 or 0.9.
5. The effect of the scoring window: transient length, beats, and windows shorter than one period.

**Deliverables.** A repo plus a 4-page paper.

</Article>

```python
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
auc = roc_auc_score(labels, scores)            # labels: 1 = bump, 0 = no bump
auc_abs = roc_auc_score(labels, np.abs(scores))
```

**Design tips:**
- Randomize the bump *phase* (the time of the bump within the drive cycle). It's what makes transients matter.
- Use several seeds per condition and report confidence intervals on AUC (bootstrap).
- Overlay the linear-theory prediction $A\,|H(i\omega)|$ before and after the bump on every amplitude plot.

**Rubric:**

1. **Clear claim:** the negative result is stated precisely, with the condition under which it holds.
2. **Theory meets simulation:** analytic predictions are overlaid on every key empirical figure.
3. **Statistical care:** seeds, confidence intervals, and no cherry-picked frequencies.
