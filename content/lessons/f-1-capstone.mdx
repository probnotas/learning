<Article id="brief" title="The brief">

**Research question.** How well does a small learned latent world model predict a MuJoCo robot's future, how quickly do its open-loop rollouts degrade, and is its prediction error a reliable body-change detector compared with a physics self-model?

**Why a lab cares.** World-model teams (1X's world model work, Dreamer-style agents) live and die by **evaluation**: rollout fidelity over horizon, failure under distribution shift, and whether model error signals anything useful. A careful evaluation study on a small, fully controlled system is the kind of work a world-model lab can read in ten minutes and immediately judge.

**What you'll build.**
1. A dataset from a **Gymnasium MuJoCo** environment (e.g. `Reacher-v5` or `HalfCheetah-v5`) with random and scripted actions: proprioception, plus optional rendered frames.
2. A world model in **PyTorch**: an encoder, a latent dynamics model (GRU / RSSM-lite) and a decoder, trained on next-state prediction.
3. Open-loop rollout error vs. horizon, compared with a linear model and a physics baseline.
4. Distribution shift: change body mass and link lengths in the MJCF and measure the jump in prediction error, plus ROC/AUC as a change detector.
5. A comparison with the A1-style physics self-model on the same detection task.

**Deliverables.** A repo plus a 5-page paper.

</Article>
