<Article id="brief" title="The brief">

**Research question.** When a robot's body changes mid-task, does a model-based controller with an explicit self-model (LQR + Kalman filter + re-identification) recover faster and more safely than a model-free RL policy, and what does each need to notice the change?

**Why a lab cares.** Every humanoid team runs both kinds of controller and argues about the trade-offs. A small, fair, reproducible head-to-head that measures *recovery after a body change*, using the Kalman filter's innovation as a built-in change detector, speaks directly to how world-model and self-model teams think.

**What you'll build.**
1. A **MuJoCo** system worth controlling: a cart-pole or reaction-wheel pendulum, or the compliant arm holding a target angle.
2. LQR from the linearized model, with a Kalman filter whose innovation is your change signal.
3. A **PPO** policy trained with Stable-Baselines3 on a **Gymnasium** wrapper of the same model.
4. Body change mid-episode (mass or link length): tracking error, recovery time, and failure rate for both.
5. Adaptation: re-identify after detection (A1 least squares) and re-solve LQR, vs. a PPO policy trained with domain randomization.

**Deliverables.** A repo plus a 5-page paper.

</Article>

**Rubric:**

1. **Fair comparison:** matched nominal performance, compute reported, multiple seeds.
2. **Change detection:** innovation-based detection evaluated (delay, false alarms).
3. **Clear results:** recovery metrics with uncertainty across change magnitudes.
4. **Insight:** when each approach wins and why, without overclaiming.
5. **Reproducibility:** training configs, seeds, saved policies.
