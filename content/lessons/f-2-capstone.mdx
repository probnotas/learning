<Article id="brief" title="The brief">

**Research question.** How much self-directed "babbling" data does a robot arm need to learn a useful self-model, and after damage, how few new samples does it need to repair that model? How does a learned self-model compare with explicit parameter identification?

**Why a lab cares.** This is the heart of self-modeling research (Bongard, Zykov & Lipson 2006; Chen et al. 2022), and of what a humanoid needs when its body changes. A small, clean reproduction with a *sample-efficiency* angle, and an honest comparison with physics-based identification, shows you understand both camps.

**What you'll build.**
1. A 3-link planar arm in **MuJoCo** (MJCF) with joint-angle sensing and end-effector position.
2. Babbling data: random joint targets, logging (joint angles, commands) → end-effector position and next state.
3. A learned self-model (a **PyTorch** MLP: forward kinematics plus one-step dynamics), with accuracy vs. amount of babbling data.
4. Damage: shorten a link, lock a joint, or weaken a motor. Detect it from self-model error, then repair with few new samples (fine-tuning vs. retraining) and plot samples-to-recover.
5. A baseline: explicit kinematic/dynamic parameter identification (A1 style) on the same data budget.

**Deliverables.** A repo plus a 5-page paper.

</Article>

<Article id="setup" title="Tools and setup">

**Tools:** MuJoCo, PyTorch, NumPy, Matplotlib.

```bash
pip install mujoco torch numpy matplotlib
```

**Starting point:** build the 3-link arm by nesting three bodies with hinge joints and `position` actuators, and add a `site` at the tip to read `d.site_xpos`. Apply damage by editing `m.geom_size` / `m.body_pos` (link length), `m.jnt_range` (locked joint) or `m.actuator_gainprm` (weak motor) between episodes.

</Article>
